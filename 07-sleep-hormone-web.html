Sleep, cortisol and the hormone web

Sleep, melatonin, cortisol, insulin and the sex hormones pull on each other in both directions. Numbers on the lines match the notes. To improve the whole web, start with sleep.

The twelve notes
1Sleep ↔ Melatonin

Each supports the other. REM sleep declines with age from mid-life. Start here.

2Sleep ↔ Cortisol

Less sleep raises ACTH and cortisol, flattens the curve and removes the overnight low. A flat curve is itself a marker of insomnia.

3Melatonin ↔ Cortisol

High cortisol means low melatonin. A flat cortisol curve gives a flat melatonin curve.

4Sleep ↔ Insulin resistance

Less sleep lowers insulin sensitivity and raises diabetes risk. Insulin resistance delays sleep onset and increases snoring.

5Insulin resistance ↔ Cortisol

More insulin raises ACTH and cortisol and disables the off switch. Cortisol impairs glucose uptake and β-cell insulin release.

6Insulin resistance → Testosterone

Raises GnRH and LH, lowers SHBG: more free testosterone, acne.

7Insulin resistance → Estrogen

Visceral fat increases aromatase, which raises estrogen.

8Testosterone ↔ Cortisol

Testosterone lowers cortisol. Chronically high cortisol lowers testosterone.

9Cortisol → Progesterone

Chronic cortisol depletes allopregnanolone and progesterone: anxiety, poorer sleep.

10Estrogen ↔ Cortisol

Estrogen raises cortisol and CRH and impairs the off switch.

11Cortisol, sleep → Mood

Structural change in the hippocampus, prefrontal cortex and amygdala.

12Sex hormones → Mood

Testosterone, progesterone and estrogen all shape mood and cognition.
