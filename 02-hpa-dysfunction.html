    The HPA response is self-limiting through negative feedback. On the right is its normal daily rhythm.

    Stage 1 · Normal

    A sharp morning rise, then a steady decline to a low at bedtime. Cortisol's own negative feedback switches off CRH once the job is done.

    Stage 2 · Acute

    Sympathetic hyperactivation drives exaggerated cortisol release. Cortisol stays high all day, yet its helpful actions begin to decline.

    Stage 3 · Chronic

    CRH stops being the driver. Output drops and the curve flattens. Arginine vasopressin (AVP) takes over, and cortisol cannot switch it off.

    Stage 4 · Exhaustion

    HPA reactivity fails and the cortisol response fades. The sympathetic system compensates with more activity: wired, but depleted.

    The curve flattens in stages

    Under chronic load the balance between the two systems shifts. Side by side, the four curves show the daily rhythm losing its shape: from a clear morning peak to a flat line.
